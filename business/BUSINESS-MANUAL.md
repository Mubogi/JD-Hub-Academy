## 1. The commercial position

### What we sell

Three things, in ascending order of margin:

1. **Cohort courses** — scheduled, facilitated, certificate-bearing. Reliable volume.
2. **Corporate training** — delivered on-site to an organisation's staff. Highest margin per day.
3. **Digital course books** — the PDFs in this repository. Near-zero marginal cost, sold at scale.

### Why people buy from us

- We teach in the tools they already own. No new hardware, no new licences.
- Every session ends with something the learner made for their own business.
- We are local, we speak the languages, and we use local examples.
- We certify against a rubric, not attendance.
- We teach judgement and verification, not just button-pushing. That is what makes a graduate employable rather than dangerous.

### The competitive reality

Free YouTube tutorials exist and will always exist. They do not:
- Give a certificate an employer recognises
- Force a deadline
- Put a facilitator in the room to catch mistakes
- Produce a portfolio
- Provide a cohort of peers

We are not selling information. We are selling completion, competence and credibility.

## 3. The unit economics

Know these numbers before you set a price.

### 3.1 A single cohort of 12

| Line | Amount |
|---|---|
| **Revenue** (12 × 350,000) | 4,200,000 |
| Venue hire (12 sessions) | (600,000) |
| Internet / data | (200,000) |
| Printing and materials | (250,000) |
| Refreshments | (400,000) |
| Facilitator fee (if not you) | (1,200,000) |
| Marketing and advertising | (300,000) |
| **Contribution before your time** | **1,250,000** |

If you facilitate it yourself, your income is 2,450,000 for roughly 24 contact
hours plus preparation — approximately 60 hours of work. That is about 40,000 per
hour. Raise it by filling the room, not by raising the price.

### 3.2 The break-even cohort size

With the costs above, break-even is **7 learners**. Never run a cohort below 7
unless it is a strategic loss-leader with a named purpose.

### 3.3 Corporate day economics

A two-day department training at 4,500,000 with facilitator, travel and materials
at 800,000 leaves 3,700,000 for 16 contact hours. That is roughly 230,000 per
contact hour — nearly six times the individual cohort rate.

**This is why corporate work matters.** One corporate day equals roughly a week of
individual teaching, at lower delivery risk.

### 3.4 Digital product economics

Marginal cost: zero. A single sale at 60,000 with payment processing at 3 percent
nets 58,200. Sell ten a month and that is 582,000 for no delivery time.

The constraint is discovery, not production. Which is why every course book ends
with a page that names the next tier.

## 4. Sales process

### 4.1 Individual learners

**Source of leads, in order of effectiveness:**
1. Referrals from past learners (highest conversion by far)
2. WhatsApp status and broadcast
3. Facebook and Instagram, targeted by location and interest
4. Google Business Profile
5. Church, community and professional groups

**The conversion sequence:**
1. Enquiry → reply within one hour with the course outline PDF and price.
2. Ask one qualifying question: "What do you most want to be able to do after this?"
3. Send the answer back with the specific module that addresses it.
4. Offer the next start date and the early-bird deadline.
5. If no response in three days, send one useful thing (a tip, a sample lesson).
6. If still nothing in ten days, close politely and add to the mailing list.

**The single biggest lever:** reply speed. Most enquirers contact three providers.
The first to reply properly wins the majority of them.

### 4.2 Corporate clients

**Who to approach:** HR managers, training officers, operations managers, and
church or NGO administrators. Not the CEO first.

**The approach:**
1. A one-page letter naming a specific problem their sector has.
2. A free 30-minute demonstration for their team, on their own work.
3. A one-page proposal with a baseline measurement commitment.
4. A pilot: one department, two days, measured.

**Never lead with a brochure.** Lead with a demonstration on their actual problem.

### 4.3 The proposal format

One page, five sections: the problem in their words, what we will do, what it will
achieve, what it costs, what we need from them. Signed acceptance at the bottom.

## 5. The AI ladder: the retention strategy that matters most

This is the commercial heart of the Academy.

A learner who takes one course is worth 350,000 once. A learner who climbs the AI
ladder is worth 2,600,000 over eighteen months, and refers others.

| Stage | Course | Price | Trigger to upgrade |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | AI-101 Foundation | 350,000 | Completes capstone |
| 2 | AI-201 Practitioner | 550,000 | Ships a team system |
| 3 | AI-301 Builder | 750,000 | Ships a tool others use |
| 4 | AI-401 Specialist | 950,000 | Completes an advisory pack |

**How to make the ladder work:**

- The final module of every AI book names the next tier explicitly. It is already written into the books.
- The capstone of each tier is the entry requirement for the next. This creates a natural reason to return.
- Alumni get a 10 percent discount on the next tier, valid 60 days.
- Alumni are invited to a quarterly clinic — free, one hour, where they bring a problem. This keeps the relationship alive between tiers.
- Alumni who complete AI-401 are offered associate facilitator work. That converts your best customers into your delivery capacity.

**The metric to watch:** what percentage of AI-101 graduates enrol in AI-201 within
90 days. If it is below 30 percent, the capstone is not creating enough ambition,
or the follow-up is too slow.

## 6. Operations

### 6.1 Cohort calendar

Run cohorts on a fixed rhythm so marketing becomes predictable:

- **Intake 1:** February
- **Intake 2:** May
- **Intake 3:** August
- **Intake 4:** November

Plus rolling two-week Office courses, which can start any Monday there are six
enrolled.

The dated schedule — every course, slot, start date and enrolment window for
2026–2027 — lives in **[COHORT-CALENDAR.md](COHORT-CALENDAR.md)**. That document
is generated from `scripts/calendar_plan.py`, which also checks that the 60-day
ladder discount can actually be honoured at each intake. Change the rhythm here
and regenerate there; do not edit dates by hand.

### 6.2 Room and equipment checklist

- [ ] Seating for the enrolled number plus two
- [ ] Power: at least one socket per two learners, plus extension leads
- [ ] Internet: tested, with a mobile hotspot backup
- [ ] Projector or large screen, tested with your laptop
- [ ] Two spare laptops and one spare phone
- [ ] Printed prompt cards
- [ ] Printed attendance and assessment sheets
- [ ] Water and a break arrangement

### 6.3 Facilitator standards

- Demonstrates live, never reads slides
- Roams during every lab; never sits at the front
- Spends no more than four minutes with one learner
- Stops the room when three learners hit the same wall
- Ends every session with "what will you do differently tomorrow?"

### 6.4 Assessment and certification

Every course is assessed against the rubric printed in its book. Pass mark 60
percent, with the practical component mandatory regardless of total score.

The certificate states: learner name, course title, contact hours, tools covered,
capstone title, date, and the facilitator's signature. Specificity is what makes it
credible to an employer.

## 7. Marketing

### 7.1 What to publish, and how often

| Asset | Frequency | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Learner result post | Weekly | Proof |
| Short teaching clip (60s) | Twice weekly | Reach |
| Course start announcement | Per intake | Conversion |
| Long-form article | Monthly | Authority |
| Alumni spotlight | Monthly | Trust |

All the visual assets you need already exist in `assets/` — covers, banners and
illustrations for every course.

### 7.2 The proof engine

The most effective marketing asset is a named learner with a specific result:
"Nakato cut her quotation time from 40 minutes to 8." One of those is worth more
than a month of advertising.

Collect one at the end of every cohort. Ask permission. Publish it.

### 7.3 Advertising

Start at 30,000–50,000 per campaign. Objective: messages. One location, one age
band, one interest. Run seven days without changing anything, then change one
thing.

Do not boost posts for likes. Likes come from people who already know you.

## 8. Financial management

### 8.1 The accounts you need

- **Income:** cohort fees, corporate, digital sales, facilitator licences
- **Direct costs:** venue, facilitator, materials, data, refreshments
- **Fixed costs:** rent, salaries, subscriptions, marketing
- **Cash:** the number that actually decides survival

### 8.2 The weekly review

Ninety minutes, same time each week. Revenue and enrolments against target, cash
position, open corporate proposals, one process improvement.

### 8.3 The buffer

Keep one month of fixed costs untouched. Education income is seasonal; the buffer
is what lets you decline a bad cohort and wait for a good one.

### 8.4 When to hire

Hire a facilitator when you are turning away more than two cohorts per intake. Not
before. A facilitator hired too early is a fixed cost against unpredictable
revenue, which is how training businesses die.

## 9. Growth path

### Year one — Prove it
Four AI-101 cohorts, rolling Office courses, one corporate client, the digital
library published. Target: 60 individual learners, 2 corporate engagements.

### Year two — Systemise
Add AI-201 and AI-301 cohorts. Two associate facilitators. A corporate retainer or
two. Target: 150 learners, 6 corporate engagements, 40 percent of revenue from
corporate.

### Year three — Scale
Full AI ladder running. Facilitator licences sold to two institutions. Digital
library as a standing product. Target: 300 learners, 12 corporate engagements,
and the Academy running without you in the room.

That last clause is the whole point. An academy that needs you in every session is
a job. An academy with licensed facilitators, published books and a certified
curriculum is an institution.
