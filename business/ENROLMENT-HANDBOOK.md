## 1. Choosing your course

### If you are new to AI

Start with **AI in Everyday Life & Business (AI-101)**. Six weeks. No prior
knowledge. You will use ChatGPT, Gemini, Meta AI and Canva on your own real work.

Do not start with a higher tier. The higher tiers assume you have already shipped
something.

### If you want to earn from design or content

- **Graphic Design** — if you want to sell design work.
- **Content Creation & Storytelling** — if you want to make video and build an audience.
- **Digital Marketing** — if you want to bring customers to a business.

These three stack. Design + Content + Marketing is a complete freelance offer.

### If you run a business

- **Business Growth & Sales Systems** — pricing, sales process, systems.
- **Digital Marketing** — finding customers affordably.
- **AI in Everyday Life** — reclaiming your own hours.

### If you work in an office or are job-seeking

- **Microsoft Office Suite** — the four-week programme covering everything.
- **Word + Excel Combo** — the two-week version if you are short on time.
- **Basic Digital Literacy** — if computers are genuinely new to you.

### If you work in a SACCO or handle money

- **Financial & SACCO Management** — bookkeeping, loan cycles, portfolio quality.
- **Microsoft Excel** — the tool you will use daily.

### If you are a parent or teacher

- **Scratch Coding** — for children 8 and up, aligned to Uganda and Cambridge.
- **Practical STEM** — hands-on electronics and robotics with cheap materials.

### The one book everyone should read

**From Scratch to President** — 52 weekly principles and tasks. Not a course, a
year-long manual. Read it alongside any other course.

## 2. How to enrol

**Step 1 — Enquire.** WhatsApp or call JD Hub Academy with the course name. You
will get the full outline and the price within one working hour.

**Step 2 — Qualify.** You will be asked one question: *what do you most want to be
able to do after this course?* Answer honestly. It shapes how you are taught.

**Step 3 — Pay.** Mobile money or bank transfer. You will receive a receipt and a
confirmation message with the start date, venue and what to bring.

**Step 4 — Prepare.** You will receive a short pre-course checklist. Complete it
before day one.

**Step 5 — Attend.** Come on time. Bring your device.

**Step 6 — Finish.** Complete the capstone, sit the assessment, receive your
certificate and your course book.

---

## 3. What you need to bring

### For most courses
- A smartphone **or** a laptop (either is enough for AI-101)
- A notebook and pen
- Your own real work to practise on — this matters more than anything else

### For Office courses
- A laptop with Microsoft Office installed (or access to Google Workspace)
- A USB flash drive

### For Scratch Coding (children)
- Nothing. We provide the computers.

### What is not included
- Devices, internet data, paid tool subscriptions, printing.

Fees are listed in the course catalogue and confirmed in writing at enrolment.

**Early bird:** 10–15 percent discount if paid more than two weeks before the

---

## 5. Attendance and completion

**Attendance.** 80 percent minimum to sit the assessment. Sessions are not
recorded for individual catch-up, so missed labs are missed practice.

**Missed sessions.** Tell your facilitator in advance if you can. You will receive
the materials and a short catch-up task.

**Completion.** You complete the course by passing the capstone rubric in your
course book. The pass mark is 60 percent, and the practical demonstration is
mandatory regardless of your total score.

**Certificates** state your name, the course, the contact hours, the tools covered,
your capstone title, and the date. They are signed by the facilitator.

## 6. What you will leave with

Every JD Hub Academy course gives you four things:

1. **A working artefact.** Something you built, for your own work, that you will
   still be using in six months.
2. **The course book.** The full PDF, yours permanently.
3. **A library.** The prompts and templates you used, saved and organised.
4. **A certificate.** Specific enough that an employer can verify what you can do.

What you will not leave with is a vague sense that you "learned about AI". That is
what other courses provide.

## 7. After the course

**The 90-day habit.** This is the difference between a course that changed
something and a course you forget.

- **Weeks 1–4:** one real task per week with what you learned. Log four things:
  the task, the prompt, the time saved, and what you had to fix.
- **Weeks 5–8:** teach one colleague one thing. Teaching is how you keep it.
- **Weeks 9–12:** automate one recurring task. Report the hours saved.

**The alumni group.** Stays active for 90 days after your course. Peer answers beat
facilitator answers, and the relationships outlast the content.

**The quarterly clinic.** Free, one hour, for all alumni. Bring one problem. This
is how you keep improving after the formal teaching ends.

**The next tier.** If you completed AI-101, AI-201 is the natural next step. The
final module of your book explains what it covers and why it comes next.
