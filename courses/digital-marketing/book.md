# Module 1 — Marketing Is Arithmetic

Most small-business marketing fails not because the owner lacks passion, but
because nobody ever did the arithmetic. They spend 50,000 shillings boosting a
post, get some likes, feel encouraged, and have no idea whether it produced a
single customer.

This course teaches marketing as a set of numbers you can measure, with creative
work inside it. The creative part is the fun part; the arithmetic is the part that
keeps you in business.

::: scripture
"For which of you, intending to build a tower, does not sit down first and count
the cost, whether he has enough to finish it?"
— Luke 14:28
:::

Count the cost. Know what a customer costs you to acquire, and what they are worth
to you. Everything else in marketing is a variation on those two numbers.

## Lesson 1.1 — The four numbers you must know

**1. Cost per lead (CPL).** Total spend ÷ number of enquiries. If you spend
100,000 and get 20 enquiries, your CPL is 5,000.

**2. Conversion rate.** Enquiries ÷ sales. If 20 enquiries produce 4 sales, your
conversion rate is 20 percent.

**3. Cost per acquisition (CPA).** Total spend ÷ sales. 100,000 ÷ 4 = 25,000.

**4. Customer lifetime value (LTV).** Average sale × purchases per year × years
they stay. A customer who buys 60,000 twice a year for three years is worth
360,000.

The only question that matters: **is CPA lower than LTV?** If yes, spend more. If
no, fix something before you spend another shilling.

## Lesson 1.2 — Why most marketing spend is wasted

Three reasons, all fixable.

**No clear audience.** If your ad is for everyone, it is for nobody, and the
platform charges you for the ambiguity.

**No offer.** "We sell quality products" is not an offer. "Order today and we
deliver free within Kampala by Friday" is an offer.

**No measurement.** If you cannot say what a customer cost you, you cannot
improve, and you cannot know when to stop.

## Lesson 1.3 — The marketing stack for a small business

You need five things, in this order of importance. Everything else is optional.

| Priority | Asset | Why first |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | WhatsApp Business | Where your customers already are |
| 2 | Google Business Profile | Free, and it is how people find local businesses |
| 3 | Facebook / Instagram page | Discovery and social proof |
| 4 | A way to capture enquiries | So nothing is lost |
| 5 | Paid ads | Only after the first four work |

Most people start at five and wonder why it is expensive. Paid ads amplify what
already works. They do not fix what does not.

::: exercise
**Exercise 1.3 — Calculate your numbers**

1. Write down your last month's marketing spend.
2. Count the enquiries it produced. Count the sales.
3. Calculate CPL, conversion rate, and CPA.
4. Estimate LTV honestly: average sale, purchases per year, years retained.
5. Answer the only question: is CPA lower than LTV?

**Time:** 25 minutes. **Deliverable:** four numbers on one page.
:::

# Module 2 — Knowing Who You Serve

## Lesson 2.1 — The buyer, specifically

A buyer definition is useful only if it changes what you do. "Women aged 25–45"
changes nothing. This changes everything:

::: box
**BUYER — The busy school parent**

Who: A mother of two primary-age children, working, lives in Ntinda, earns a
modest salary.

What she is trying to do: keep the children occupied and learning during the
holiday without spending a fortune.

What she fears: paying for a programme where the children sit idle; being
embarrassed by a badly run outfit; the cost running over.

Where she is: WhatsApp mum groups, Facebook, and the school gate.

What she trusts: another parent's recommendation, and photographs of children
actually doing something.

What she will pay: 150,000–250,000 for two weeks, if she can see the schedule.
:::

Now every marketing decision has an answer. Where to advertise, what photograph
to use, which objection to address first, what price to test.

## Lesson 2.2 — Positioning

Positioning is one sentence: **for [who], we are the [what] that [difference],
because [proof].**

::: box
For working parents in Ntinda, we are the holiday programme that keeps children
genuinely busy, because every day has a printed schedule and parents get
photographs by 5 p.m.
:::

The proof clause is what makes it credible. Without proof, positioning is a
slogan.

## Lesson 2.3 — The objection list

Write the five reasons people do not buy from you. Then address them, in order,
in your marketing. Most businesses answer objections nobody has and ignore the
real ones.

Common ones: too expensive, not sure it works, never heard of you, can I trust
you with my money, what if I need to change my mind.

::: exercise
**Exercise 2.3 — Write your buyer and positioning**

1. Fill in the buyer template for your real business.
2. Write the positioning sentence, with a proof clause you can actually deliver.
3. List your five real objections. Use actual conversations, not guesses.
4. For each, write one sentence you could say in marketing to answer it.

**Time:** 40 minutes. **Deliverable:** one page you will use for every campaign.
:::

# Module 3 — The Assets You Set Up Once

## Lesson 3.1 — WhatsApp Business, done properly

This is the highest-return hour in the whole course for most small businesses.

- **Business profile:** real name, category, address, hours, website, email.
- **Catalogue:** your products with prices and photographs.
- **Greeting message:** sent automatically to new contacts.
- **Away message:** sent outside hours, with an expectation of when you reply.
- **Quick replies:** your ten most common answers, saved with shortcuts.
- **Labels:** new enquiry, quoted, paid, delivered, follow-up.

That last one — labels — is the difference between a chat app and a sales system.
Without labels, you cannot tell how many enquiries you have, and you cannot
follow up, and follow-up is where most sales are actually made.

## Lesson 3.2 — Google Business Profile

Free, and for local businesses it is often the single largest source of new
customers.

- Claim it and verify it.
- Correct category, address, phone, hours.
- Ten real photographs, updated seasonally.
- A short description with your location and service, in plain words people
  actually search.
- Ask every satisfied customer for a review. Twenty reviews will outperform most
  advertising budgets.

## Lesson 3.3 — Facebook and Instagram, the minimum

- Profile photograph: logo, legible at thumbnail size.
- Cover image: what you do, in one line of text on the image.
- Bio: who you serve, what you sell, where, and how to contact you.
- Pinned post: your best offer, or your best proof.
- Contact button: WhatsApp, not a contact form.

::: warn
**Do not buy followers.** It damages your reach permanently, because the platform
learns that your audience does not engage, and then shows your content to fewer
real people. It is the most expensive cheap thing you can do.
:::

::: exercise
**Exercise 3.3 — Complete the setup**

1. Fix WhatsApp Business: profile, catalogue, greeting, away message, ten quick
   replies, five labels.
2. Claim and complete Google Business Profile with ten photographs.
3. Fix the Facebook page: photo, cover, bio, pinned post, WhatsApp button.
4. Ask five happy customers for reviews this week.

**Time:** 90 minutes. **Deliverable:** all three assets complete and verified.
:::

# Module 4 — The Offer

## Lesson 4.1 — Why offers beat advertising

A weak offer with strong advertising loses money. A strong offer with no
advertising still spreads by word of mouth.

An offer has four parts: **what** you get, **what it costs**, **what risk is
removed**, and **why now**.

::: box
**Weak:** "We do graphic design. Contact us for a quote."

**Strong:** "A complete poster set — A3 poster, Instagram post, WhatsApp status,
and a flyer — for 120,000 shillings, delivered in 48 hours. If you do not like the
first draft, you pay nothing."
:::

The strong version answers: what exactly, how much, how fast, and what if it goes
wrong.

## Lesson 4.2 — Risk reversal

The single most powerful element. You are not discounting; you are removing the
customer's fear.

Options, in order of cost to you:

1. **A clear guarantee** ("if it is late, delivery is free").
2. **A small first step** ("start with one post, not a package").
3. **Proof** ("here are three customers who did this").
4. **A trial** ("first design free, pay if you use it").

## Lesson 4.3 — Urgency that is honest

Fake countdowns and invented deadlines destroy trust the moment they are noticed,
and they are always noticed.

Honest urgency: a real class date, a real stock level, a real seasonal deadline, a
real capacity limit. If you do not have a real one, do not invent one — instead
make the offer better.

::: exercise
**Exercise 4.3 — Write your offer**

1. Write your offer in the four-part structure.
2. Add one risk reversal you can afford.
3. Find one honest reason to act now.
4. Test it on three people. Ask only: "What is unclear?"

**Time:** 35 minutes. **Deliverable:** one offer, tested on three people.
:::

# Module 5 — Paid Advertising Without Burning Money

## Lesson 5.1 — The first campaign

Start with 30,000–50,000 shillings. Not more. You are buying information, not
customers, in week one.

The structure that works for a small local business:

| Setting | Choice | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Objective | Messages or Leads | You want conversations, not likes |
| Audience | One location, one age band, one interest | Narrow beats broad for small budgets |
| Placement | Facebook and Instagram feed | Cheapest, most reliable |
| Budget | Small daily amount for 5–7 days | Enough data to learn |
| Creative | One video or three images, same offer | Test the creative, not everything |
| Call to action | "Send message" | Lowest friction |

## Lesson 5.2 — Reading the results honestly

After seven days, look at four numbers: reach, clicks or messages, cost per
message, and how many became customers.

If cost per message is high, the creative or the audience is wrong.
If messages are cheap but nobody buys, the offer or the response speed is wrong.
If nothing happens at all, the audience is too narrow or the budget too small to
exit the learning phase.

## Lesson 5.3 — The three things that fix a failing campaign

1. **Change the first three seconds of the creative.** Most failures are hooks.
2. **Narrow or widen the audience.** One change, then wait.
3. **Improve the offer.** Discounting is the last resort, not the first.

::: warn
**Never boost a post just because it got likes.** Organic likes come from people
who already follow you. Paid reach is for people who do not. They are different
audiences and they respond differently.
:::

::: exercise
**Exercise 5.3 — Run one small campaign**

1. Set up the campaign exactly as specified.
2. Run it for seven days without changing anything. (This is the hard part.)
3. Record the four numbers.
4. Change exactly one thing. Run again.
5. Compare. Write down what you learned.

**Time:** 30 minutes setup, one week running.
**Deliverable:** two campaigns, compared with numbers.
:::

# Module 6 — The Funnel and Where It Leaks

## Lesson 6.1 — The five stages

**See → Click → Enquire → Buy → Return.**

Every business leaks somewhere. Finding the leak is worth more than any new
campaign, because you are fixing a problem you already paid for.

| Stage | Typical leak | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| See | Wrong audience | Narrow the targeting |
| Click | Weak hook | Rewrite the first three seconds |
| Enquire | Slow reply | Respond within one hour |
| Buy | Unclear price or trust | Publish prices, add proof |
| Return | No follow-up | A simple thank-you and a reminder |

## Lesson 6.2 — Response time is the cheapest win available

In most small businesses, the largest single leak is reply speed. A customer who
waits four hours has often already bought elsewhere.

Set an away message, set a target (one hour during business hours), and use your
quick replies. This costs nothing and typically produces the biggest improvement
of any change in this course.

## Lesson 6.3 — Follow-up, where most sales live

Most enquiries do not buy on first contact. Most businesses never follow up once.

A three-touch sequence that is not annoying:

1. **Day 1:** answer the question, give the price, ask if they would like to proceed.
2. **Day 3:** one useful thing — a photograph, a review, a delivery date.
3. **Day 10:** a polite close: "I will assume the timing is not right — message me
   whenever it is."

The third message alone recovers a surprising number of sales, because it removes
the pressure and gives the customer an easy way back.

::: exercise
**Exercise 6.3 — Find your leak**

1. Trace your last twenty enquiries through the five stages.
2. Count how many dropped at each stage.
3. Identify the single biggest leak.
4. Fix only that one, this week.
5. Measure again next month.

**Time:** 45 minutes. **Deliverable:** a leak identified and fixed.
:::

# Module 7 — Content and Ads Working Together

## Lesson 7.1 — The division of labour

Organic content builds trust over time. Paid advertising buys attention now.
Using one to do the other's job is why both feel expensive.

**Organic:** proof, personality, education, behind-the-scenes. Its job is that
when someone sees your ad, they recognise you.

**Paid:** a specific offer to a specific audience with a specific action. Its job
is to produce enquiries this week.

## Lesson 7.2 — Retargeting, simply

Someone who watched half your video or visited your page is far more likely to buy
than a stranger. Retargeting is showing them a second message.

Even on a small budget: run one campaign to a cold audience, and a second, smaller
campaign to people who engaged. The second is usually the profitable one.

## Lesson 7.3 — The content that supports selling

Three kinds of content make paid ads work better:

1. **Proof content** — customer results, before-and-after, testimonials.
2. **Objection content** — "is it worth the price?", "how long does it take?".
3. **Process content** — showing how you work, which builds trust faster than
   claims.

::: exercise
**Exercise 7.3 — Build the support set**

1. Create three proof pieces and three objection pieces.
2. Publish them organically over two weeks.
3. Then run your next paid campaign.
4. Compare cost per message to your previous campaign.

**Time:** 60 minutes plus two weeks. **Deliverable:** a before-and-after cost per
message.
:::

# Module 8 — Email, WhatsApp Broadcasts and Retention

## Lesson 8.1 — The list you already have

Your WhatsApp contacts and past customers are the most valuable marketing asset
you own, and most businesses never use them.

Build a simple list: name, what they bought, when, and permission to message.
Never message people who did not agree — it is both ineffective and, under
data-protection law, unlawful.

## Lesson 8.2 — The broadcast that does not get you blocked

Rules that keep your list alive:

- One message a week maximum.
- Every message has something useful in it, not just a promotion.
- Short. Three sentences and a photograph.
- A clear way to stop receiving.

## Lesson 8.3 — Retention economics

Acquiring a new customer costs several times more than selling again to an
existing one. Yet most small businesses spend everything on acquisition.

Three cheap retention habits: a thank-you message after purchase, a reminder when
a consumable is likely to run out, and a genuine check-in after delivery.

::: exercise
**Exercise 8.3 — Reactivate**

1. List your last thirty customers with contact details and purchase dates.
2. Send a genuine, non-sales message to ten of them.
3. Send a relevant offer to the five most likely to buy again.
4. Record how many respond and how many buy.

**Time:** 40 minutes. **Deliverable:** a measured reactivation result.
:::

# Module 9 — Using AI Across the Whole Funnel

## Lesson 9.1 — Where AI saves real money

| Funnel stage | AI use | Saving |
|---|---|---|
| Buyer research | Synthesise reviews, competitors, questions | Hours of reading |
| Creative | Ten hook variants, three ad copies | Design time |
| Assets | Backgrounds, illustrations, resizing | Design cost |
| Response | Draft replies, quick replies, follow-ups | Response time |
| Analysis | Summarise campaign results into plain language | Reporting time |
| Retention | Personalised reminders and check-ins | Consistency |

## Lesson 9.2 — Where AI must not decide

Do not let AI decide your price, your positioning, or what you promise. Those are
strategic judgements with consequences, and the model has no stake in the outcome.

## Lesson 9.3 — The prompt that analyses a campaign

```prompt
Here are my campaign results for two weeks: spend, reach, messages, sales,
and the three creatives used.

Analyse them for a non-technical business owner:
1. Which creative produced the cheapest messages, and why might that be?
2. What is my cost per sale, and how does it compare to my customer lifetime
   value of {ltv}?
3. Name the single biggest problem and the one change to test next.
4. Do not invent numbers not in the data.
```

::: exercise
**Exercise 9.3 — Automate the reporting**

1. Put your campaign numbers in a sheet.
2. Run the prompt above.
3. Check every figure it quotes against your sheet.
4. Correct anything it got wrong, then save the prompt to your library.

**Time:** 25 minutes. **Deliverable:** a repeatable campaign review.
:::

# Module 10 — Capstone: A 90-Day Marketing Plan

## Lesson 10.1 — The brief

Produce a one-page, 90-day plan with: buyer, positioning, offer, the assets you
will fix, one paid campaign per month, a content series, a follow-up sequence, and
the numbers you will measure.

## Lesson 10.2 — The rubric

| Criterion | Weight | Excellent | Weak |
|---|---|---|---|
| Numbers literacy | 25% | CPL, CPA, LTV calculated and used in decisions | No numbers |
| Offer strength | 25% | Clear, risk-reversed, honest urgency | "Contact us for a quote" |
| Funnel diagnosis | 20% | Identified and fixed the real leak | Blames the algorithm |
| Execution | 20% | Campaigns actually run, results recorded | Plan only |
| Ethics | 10% | No fake urgency, no bought followers, honest claims | Shortcuts |

::: scripture
"A false balance is an abomination to the Lord, but a just weight is His delight."
— Proverbs 11:1
:::

Honest measurement and honest claims are the same virtue. A business built on
inflated numbers has to keep inflating them. A business built on real ones only
has to keep improving.
