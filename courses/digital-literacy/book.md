# Module 1 — Starting From Zero

This course assumes nothing. Not that you have used a computer. Not that you
type quickly. Not that you are young. It assumes only that you have decided to
learn something you were not taught, which is more than most people ever do.

If you feel embarrassed, put that down. The embarrassment is not information. It
is a habit of comparing yourself to people who started earlier, and it costs you
nothing to ignore.

::: scripture
"The fear of the Lord is the beginning of wisdom, and the knowledge of the Holy
One is understanding."
— Proverbs 9:10
:::

Wisdom begins with honesty about what you do not know. That is where you are
standing right now, and it is the correct place to stand.

## Lesson 1.1 — What the machine is

Four parts. That is all.

**The screen** shows you what is happening.
**The keyboard and mouse** (or the touchscreen) let you give instructions.
**The processor and memory** do the work.
**The storage** keeps things when the power goes off.

Everything else — the internet, apps, printers — connects to these four.

## Lesson 1.2 — Turning it on, and off

Turn on: press the power button and wait. Waiting is part of it; a computer that
is still starting will ignore you, and pressing buttons impatiently is how people
conclude that "it is broken".

Turn off properly: use the shutdown option in the menu. Do not simply switch off
the power. That is like stopping a car by removing the key at speed — sometimes it
is fine, sometimes it destroys something.

## Lesson 1.3 — The mouse and the touchscreen

**Click** — press once, gently. Selects something.
**Double-click** — press twice quickly. Opens something.
**Right-click** — opens a menu of options. This is the most under-used action by
beginners, and it is where half the useful options live.
**Drag** — hold and move. Moves things.

On a phone, the equivalents: **tap** (click), **double-tap** (zoom),
**long-press** (right-click), **swipe** (scroll).

::: exercise
**Exercise 1.3 — The five actions**

1. Open any folder or app on your device.
2. Click something once. What happened?
3. Double-click something. What happened?
4. Right-click (or long-press) on empty space. Read every option.
5. Drag something from one place to another.

**Time:** 15 minutes. **Deliverable:** you can name what each action does.
:::

# Module 2 — Files, Folders and Finding Things

## Lesson 2.1 — The filing cabinet idea

A file is a document, a photo, a video — anything you saved.
A folder is a container for files.
Folders can go inside folders, like boxes inside boxes.

This is not complicated, but it is the skill that separates people who are
comfortable with computers from people who are frightened of them. The frightened
ones cannot find anything, so they save everything to the desktop, and then the
desktop becomes chaos.

## Lesson 2.2 — A folder structure that works

Three levels is enough. Not five.

```
My Work
  Clients
    2026
      Client A
      Client B
  Money
    Invoices
    Receipts
  Personal
    Documents
    Photos
```

Name files so that they sort usefully: **date, then subject**.
`2026-03-14 Invoice 042 Mukasa.pdf` sorts correctly and tells you what it is
without opening it.

## Lesson 2.3 — Search

You do not need to remember where a file is. You need to remember what it is
called. Search by name, and on most systems also by content.

The lesson: name things well, and searching becomes reliable. Name things badly
("document1", "IMG_2847") and you will spend years hunting.

::: exercise
**Exercise 2.3 — Build your cabinet**

1. Create a folder called "My Work" in your documents.
2. Inside it, create three folders: Clients, Money, Personal.
3. Move ten files from your desktop into the correct folders.
4. Rename five of them using the date-subject pattern.
5. Use search to find one of them by name.

**Time:** 30 minutes. **Deliverable:** a folder structure with ten organised files.
:::

# Module 3 — Email Without Fear

## Lesson 3.1 — The parts of an email

**To** — who receives it. **Cc** — who is informed. **Bcc** — who receives it
without the others seeing. **Subject** — what it is about. **Body** — the message.
**Attachment** — a file sent with it.

Two rules that prevent most embarrassment: check the recipient before you send,
and always write a subject.

## Lesson 3.2 — Writing a professional email

Four parts: greeting, purpose in one sentence, detail if needed, clear close.

::: box
**Subject:** Quotation request — 20 chairs

Dear Mr Mukasa,

I would like a quotation for 20 plastic chairs, delivered to Ntinda, by
30 March.

Please include the delivery cost and the payment terms.

Thank you,
Nakato Sarah
07xx xxx xxx
:::

Short. Specific. One ask. That is a professional email, and it takes ninety
seconds.

## Lesson 3.3 — Attachments and the size problem

To attach: use the paperclip icon, choose the file, wait for it to upload before
sending.

If the file is too large, compress it, or use a link (Google Drive, WhatsApp). Do
not send a 40MB video by email; it will fail and you will not know.

## Lesson 3.4 — Recognising a scam

::: warn
**Five signs an email is not what it claims.**

1. It creates urgency: "your account will be closed in 24 hours".
2. It asks for a password, PIN, or payment.
3. The sender's address is slightly wrong (not the real company domain).
4. It greets you generically: "Dear Customer".
5. It contains a link you did not expect, asking you to log in.

When in doubt: do not click. Open the company's website yourself and log in
there, or phone the number you already have.
:::

::: exercise
**Exercise 3.4 — Send three real emails**

1. Send a professional email requesting something you actually need.
2. Send an email with an attachment.
3. Reply to an email, keeping the thread.
4. Find one suspicious email in your inbox and identify why it is suspicious.

**Time:** 30 minutes. **Deliverable:** three sent emails.
:::

# Module 4 — Searching the Internet Properly

## Lesson 4.1 — How search works, briefly

A search engine does not know the answer. It finds pages that match your words
and ranks them by how useful other people found them. Your job is to give it good
words.

## Lesson 4.2 — Better searches

| Instead of | Try |
|---|---|
| "chairs" | "plastic chairs wholesale Kampala price" |
| "how to register business" | "URA business registration requirements sole proprietor" |
| "cheap phone" | "phone under 500000 UGX Kampala" |
| A whole question | The three or four words that would appear on the page you want |

Add a year to get current results. Use quotation marks to search an exact phrase.

## Lesson 4.3 — Judging a source

Five questions before you believe anything:

1. **Who wrote it?** A named person or organisation, or anonymous?
2. **When?** Old information is often wrong information.
3. **Why?** Are they selling something?
4. **Does anyone else say it?** One source is a claim; three are a fact.
5. **Is it specific?** Real numbers and names, or vague generalities?

::: warn
**The forward that costs people money.** A message forwarded on WhatsApp is not
evidence. Most financial scams, fake job offers and fake health cures spread
exactly this way, and they spread because they are forwarded by people you trust,
who also did not check.
:::

::: exercise
**Exercise 4.3 — Verify a claim**

1. Find a claim forwarded to you on WhatsApp or social media.
2. Search for it, adding the word "false" or "fact check".
3. Check two independent sources.
4. Write one sentence: is it true, false, or unproven?
5. Send that sentence back to whoever forwarded it. Politely.

**Time:** 25 minutes. **Deliverable:** one verified claim.
:::

# Module 5 — Documents and Spreadsheets

## Lesson 5.1 — The word processor

Five things, and you can produce any document:

1. **Type** and edit.
2. **Bold**, *italic*, underline — sparingly.
3. **Headings** — use the built-in styles, not just bigger text.
4. **Tables** — insert, add rows, adjust columns.
5. **Save as PDF** — so it looks the same on any device.

## Lesson 5.2 — The spreadsheet

A spreadsheet is a grid of cells. Each cell has an address: column letter, row
number. B2 is column B, row 2.

Three things make it powerful:

**Formulas** start with `=` and calculate. `=B2*C2` multiplies them.
**Functions** do common jobs. `=SUM(B2:B10)` adds a range.
**Charts** turn numbers into a picture.

## Lesson 5.3 — Your first useful sheet

A simple expense tracker. Date, item, amount, category. Then `=SUM` at the bottom.
Then a chart.

That is genuinely useful, and it takes twenty minutes.

::: exercise
**Exercise 5.3 — Build the tracker**

1. Create a sheet with columns: Date, Item, Amount, Category.
2. Enter ten real expenses from the past month.
3. Add `=SUM(D2:D11)` at the bottom.
4. Create a chart.
5. Save it and use it next month.

**Time:** 40 minutes. **Deliverable:** a working expense tracker.
:::

# Module 6 — Staying Safe Online

## Lesson 6.1 — Passwords

Four rules, and they are not optional.

1. **Long beats complicated.** Four unrelated words is stronger than "P@ssw0rd".
2. **Different for each important account.** Especially email and money.
3. **Never share them.** Not with anyone claiming to be support.
4. **Use two-step verification** on email, mobile money, and banking.

## Lesson 6.2 — The scams that actually catch people

| Scam | How it works | Defence |
|---|---|---|
| Mobile money | "You have received money, send it back" — the original transfer is reversed | Check your own balance, never trust a message |
| Fake job | You pay a "registration fee" | Real employers never charge to hire |
| Fake loan | Upfront fee for a loan that never arrives | No legitimate lender takes a fee first |
| Account takeover | A "friend" asks for a code sent to your phone | Never share a code, ever |
| Romance/emergency | Urgency, secrecy, money | Anyone rushing you is not on your side |

## Lesson 6.3 — The one rule

**Nobody legitimate will ever ask you for a password, a PIN, or a code sent to
your phone.** Not your bank. Not your employer. Not your friend. Not support.

If someone does, it is a scam, and the conversation is over.

::: exercise
**Exercise 6.3 — Secure three accounts**

1. Change your email password to four random words.
2. Turn on two-step verification for email.
3. Do the same for mobile money or banking.
4. Write your recovery codes on paper, and keep the paper somewhere safe — not in
   your phone.

**Time:** 30 minutes. **Deliverable:** three secured accounts.
:::

# Module 7 — Doing Something Real

## Lesson 7.1 — The complete task

The test of digital literacy is not knowing what a button does. It is completing
a real task end to end.

Three tasks that count:

**Register something.** A business name, a course, a licence. Find the
requirements, prepare the documents, submit.

**Apply for something.** A job, a school place, a grant. Write the application,
attach the documents, send it correctly.

**Pay for something.** A bill, a service, an order. Do it safely and keep the
proof.

## Lesson 7.2 — Keeping proof

For every important transaction: keep the confirmation, the reference number, and
a screenshot. Save them in a folder called "Proof".

This single habit will save you an enormous amount of trouble. Organisations lose
records; the person with a screenshot wins the argument.

::: exercise
**Exercise 7.2 — Complete one real task**

1. Choose one of the three tasks above that you actually need.
2. Find out what is required. Write the list.
3. Gather the documents.
4. Complete it.
5. Save the proof in your folder.

**Time:** 90 minutes. **Deliverable:** a completed real task and the proof.
:::

# Module 8 — Capstone

## Lesson 8.1 — The brief

Demonstrate, to a facilitator, all of the following in one sitting:

- Create a folder structure and file a document correctly.
- Send an email with a correctly named attachment.
- Find information online and verify it with two sources.
- Build a spreadsheet with a formula and a chart.
- Show two-step verification enabled on your email.
- Explain one scam you now recognise.

## Lesson 8.2 — The rubric

| Criterion | Weight | Standard |
|---|---|---|
| File management | 20% | Correct structure, sensible names |
| Email | 20% | Professional, correct attachment |
| Search & verification | 20% | Two independent sources checked |
| Spreadsheet | 20% | Working formula, readable chart |
| Safety | 20% | Two-step on, scams identified |
