# Preface — Why This Book Exists

I have watched a great many people wait.

They wait for capital. They wait for the right connection. They wait for
permission, for a qualification, for the economy to improve, for someone to
notice them. Years pass. The waiting becomes the work, and the work never starts.

And I have watched a much smaller number of people begin with almost nothing —
a phone, a skill, a room — and build something that employs others, that feeds
families, that outlives them. The difference was never luck, and it was rarely
capital. It was a set of decisions made in an order.

This book is that order.

It is not a motivational book. Motivation is cheap and evaporates by Thursday.
What follows is mechanical: fifty-two principles, each paired with a task you can
complete today. Do the tasks in sequence and you will have built, within a year,
the foundation of a life that produces rather than consumes.

I wrote it because I needed it. I built systems for schools, clinics, SACCOs and
shops across Uganda, often for people who had been told their whole lives that
this kind of work was for other people. It is not. It is for anyone willing to do
the unglamorous things in the right order.

::: scripture
"Unless the Lord builds the house, they labour in vain who build it."
— Psalm 127:1
:::

That verse is not a reason to wait. It is a warning about the *manner* of
building. Effort without God is vanity. God without effort is presumption. The
whole book lives in the space between those two errors.

## How to use this book

**Read one principle per week.** Not more. The tasks take time, and the sequence
matters more than the speed.

**Do the task before the next principle.** Reading without doing produces a
pleasant feeling and no change. The task is the book; the text is only the
explanation.

**Keep a notebook.** One page per week: the principle, what you did, what happened.
After a year you will have a record of your own building that is more valuable
than any advice anyone can give you.

**Re-read it annually.** You will be a different person, and the same words will
land differently. The principle you skimmed in week nine will be the one that
saves you in year three.

## A word about money

You will notice that this book talks about money constantly. That is deliberate,
and it is not greed.

Money is stored work. It is what you get when you solve a problem for someone and
they value the solution enough to pay for it. A person who cannot manage money
cannot build anything, because every institution — a school, a clinic, a church,
a household — runs on the same arithmetic. Learning the arithmetic is not a
compromise of your values. It is what makes your values affordable.

The goal is not a number. The goal is a position: the position where your
decisions are your own, where you can say no to work that harms you, where the
people who depend on you are safe. That position is what the word *president*
means in this book — not a title, but a place of standing, authority and
responsibility.

# Part One — The Foundation

*Weeks 1–8: the habits and the honesty that everything else is built on.*

# Week 1 — Start Where You Are

Nobody begins at the beginning. Everyone begins somewhere in the middle, with
whatever they happen to have: a phone, a debt, a skill, a family obligation, a
half-finished qualification. The first discipline is refusing to wait for better
conditions.

Every person you admire started with less than they needed. That is not a
coincidence; it is the mechanism. Resources follow clarity, not the other way
around.

::: scripture
"Whatever your hand finds to do, do it with your might."
— Ecclesiastes 9:10
:::

Not "whatever you wish you had found". Whatever your hand *finds*. The work that
is in front of you, with the tools that are in your hand.

## The principle

**Begin with what you have, where you are, today.** The gap between where you are
and where you want to be is closed by action, not by preparation.

## The task

::: exercise
**Week 1 task — The inventory**

Write three lists, on paper, today.

1. **What I have.** Skills, tools, relationships, time, knowledge, physical
   assets. Be embarrassingly literal. "A phone. Two hours a day. I can drive. I
   know how to price a job."
2. **What I owe.** Money, obligations, promises, time commitments.
3. **What I can sell this month**, using only list one, without acquiring
   anything new.

Then do one thing from list three, this week. One. Not a plan for it. The thing.

**Time:** 45 minutes for the lists. The rest of the week for the action.
:::

Most people discover something uncomfortable in this exercise: they have more
than they thought, and they have been waiting for a reason that does not survive
being written down.

# Week 2 — The Honest Ledger

You cannot improve a number you refuse to look at. This week is the least
pleasant and the most important in the book.

Most people in financial difficulty do not know their actual position. They know
approximately, and approximately is where fear lives. Fear of the number is
almost always worse than the number.

::: scripture
"Buy the truth, and do not sell it."
— Proverbs 23:23
:::

Buy the truth. It costs you your self-image, and it is the only purchase in this
book that pays for everything else.

## The principle

**Know your exact position, in numbers, before you make any plan.** Clarity is
the first form of capital.

## The task

::: exercise
**Week 2 task — The honest ledger**

1. Write every amount of money you have access to today: cash, mobile money,
   bank, anything owed to you.
2. Write every amount you owe: loans, debts, obligations, unpaid bills.
3. Subtract. Write the number down. Do not round it, do not soften it.
4. Write, beside it, the date.
5. Then write one sentence: "This is where I am starting."

Do not attempt to fix anything this week. Only look.

**Time:** 90 minutes.
:::

Almost everyone finds that the number is survivable. That is the discovery. The
paralysis was never about the amount; it was about the not-knowing.

# Week 3 — The Discipline of Small

Big things are built from small things repeated. This is not a slogan; it is the
only mechanism by which anything large has ever been constructed.

A person who saves 5,000 shillings a week for five years has 1.3 million
shillings and, more importantly, has proved something about themselves that a
windfall never could. The habit is the asset. The money is a side effect.

::: scripture
"For who has despised the day of small things?"
— Zechariah 4:10
:::

The answer, historically, is almost everyone. And almost everyone stays where
they are.

## The principle

**Consistency at a small scale beats intensity at an irregular scale.** Design
for the smallest version you will actually do every week.

## The task

::: exercise
**Week 3 task — Set your floor**

Choose one habit and set its floor so low you cannot fail.

- Saving: an amount so small it is not a decision.
- Learning: ten minutes, not an hour.
- Selling: one conversation a day, not ten.

Write the floor. Do it for seven consecutive days. Mark each day on paper.

If you miss a day, do not restart. Continue the count and note the miss. The
streak is not the point; the return is.

**Time:** 10 minutes a day.
:::

# Week 4 — Time Is the Only Inventory

Money can be borrowed, replaced, and earned again. Time cannot. Every hour you
spend is gone, and the only question is whether it bought something you wanted.

Most people have never counted their hours. They know they are busy. Busy is not
the same as productive, and the difference between the two is where entire
careers are decided.

::: scripture
"So teach us to number our days, that we may gain a heart of wisdom."
— Psalm 90:12
:::

Number your days. Not vaguely — number them.

## The principle

**What you spend your hours on is what you are actually building**, regardless of
what you say you are building.

## The task

::: exercise
**Week 4 task — The hour audit**

For three days, write down what you did in each hour. Not what you intended.
What you did.

Then classify each hour: **Builds** (produces income, skill or asset),
**Maintains** (necessary but produces nothing), **Leaks** (neither).

Total the hours in each column. Most people find four to eight hours a week in
Leaks, and are astonished.

Then choose one leak and remove it for a week. Replace it with one hour of
building.

**Time:** 3 days of noting, 60 minutes of analysis.
:::

# Week 5 — Skill Before Scale

You cannot scale what you cannot do well. The order is: learn the skill, do it
well enough that people pay, then build a system around it.

The temptation is to skip to the system — the branding, the website, the team —
before the skill is worth paying for. That is how people spend money advertising
something that does not yet work.

::: scripture
"Do you see a man who excels in his work? He will stand before kings; he will not
stand before unknown men."
— Proverbs 22:29
:::

Excellence is the credential. Not certificates. The work.

## The principle

**Become genuinely good at one thing before you attempt to multiply it.**

## The task

::: exercise
**Week 5 task — The one skill**

1. Name the one skill that, if you were twice as good at it, would change your
   income most.
2. Write what "twice as good" would look like, concretely. What would you be able
   to do that you cannot do now?
3. Find the fastest route to practice: a person to learn from, a project to do,
   a course that ends in a deliverable.
4. Book 5 hours this week. Put them in the calendar as appointments.
5. Do the first hour today.

**Time:** 5 hours this week, and every week for twelve weeks.
:::

# Week 6 — The Reputation Ledger

Your reputation is a ledger of promises kept. It compounds faster than money and
collapses faster than either.

A person known for finishing what they say they will finish will be given
opportunities that more talented people never see. This is not fair. It is simply
true, and it is available to anyone.

::: scripture
"A good name is to be chosen rather than great riches, loving favour rather than
silver and gold."
— Proverbs 22:1
:::

## The principle

**Every promise is a deposit or a withdrawal.** Small promises, kept, build the
capital that large opportunities require.

## The task

::: exercise
**Week 6 task — Audit your promises**

1. List every commitment you currently have outstanding: to customers, family,
   colleagues, yourself.
2. Mark each: **kept**, **in progress**, **broken**, **forgotten**.
3. Contact every person in the broken and forgotten columns. Today. Not with an
   excuse — with a new, specific, achievable commitment.
4. From now on, before making any promise, ask one question: "Will I actually do
   this?" If the answer is not clearly yes, say no.

**Time:** 60 minutes, plus the difficult messages.
:::

# Week 7 — Say No to Good Things

The enemy of the excellent is not the terrible. It is the merely good. A mediocre
opportunity, accepted, occupies the space a great one needed.

Every successful person you can name has a longer list of things they declined
than things they accepted. This is not modesty; it is capacity management.

::: scripture
"No one engaged in warfare entangles himself with the affairs of this life, that
he may please him who enlisted him as a soldier."
— 2 Timothy 2:4
:::

## The principle

**Capacity is finite. Protect it from good opportunities so it is available for
great ones.**

## The task

::: exercise
**Week 7 task — The refusal practice**

1. List every commitment currently on your calendar and in your workload.
2. For each, ask: "If this were proposed to me today, would I accept it?"
3. For every "no", write the refusal message. Send at least two this week.
4. Write your criteria for future acceptance — three questions a new opportunity
   must pass. Keep it where you will see it.

**Time:** 45 minutes.
:::

# Week 8 — The First Hundred Days

Momentum is a real asset and it decays. The first hundred days of any new venture
set the pattern that will be very hard to change later.

The pattern to set is: decide quickly, act visibly, measure honestly, adjust
without drama.

::: scripture
"Let your 'Yes' be 'Yes,' and your 'No,' 'No.'"
— Matthew 5:37
:::

## The principle

**Set the pattern early, because patterns outlive intentions.**

## The task

::: exercise
**Week 8 task — Write your hundred-day plan**

One page.

1. **The outcome** — what will be true in 100 days that is not true now? One
   sentence, measurable.
2. **The three actions** — what you will do every week to get there.
3. **The measure** — the number you will check every Friday.
4. **The stop rule** — what evidence would make you stop or change course.

Date it. Sign it. Put it where you will see it daily.

**Time:** 60 minutes.
:::

# Part Two — Money

*Weeks 9–16: pricing, cash, debt, and the arithmetic that decides survival.*

# Week 9 — Price Your Work Properly

Underpricing is the most common form of self-harm in small business. It feels
humble. It is actually a decision to work harder for less, which eventually
destroys the business and the person running it.

Price is a signal. A price that is too low tells the customer the work is not
valuable, and they will treat it accordingly.

::: scripture
"A labourer is worthy of his wages."
— Luke 10:7
:::

Worthy. Not "grateful for whatever is offered". Worthy.

## The principle

**Your price must cover your cost, your time, your risk and your reinvestment —
or the business is consuming you.**

## The task

::: exercise
**Week 9 task — Cost your work honestly**

1. Take your most common product or service.
2. Calculate the true cost: materials, transport, tools, and your own time at a
   wage you would accept from an employer.
3. Add a margin for risk and reinvestment. Twenty percent is a floor, not a target.
4. Compare the result to your current price.
5. If you are below the floor, write the new price and the date you will apply it.

**Time:** 60 minutes.
:::

# Week 10 — Cash Is Not Profit

Businesses do not die of unprofitability. They die of running out of cash. The
distinction has bankrupted thousands of people who were, on paper, doing well.

Profit is an opinion formed by accounting rules. Cash is a fact that arrives in
your account or does not.

::: scripture
"The plans of the diligent lead surely to plenty, but those of everyone who is
hasty, surely to poverty."
— Proverbs 21:5
:::

## The principle

**Manage cash weekly; manage profit monthly.** Cash is the constraint that kills.

## The task

::: exercise
**Week 10 task — The thirteen-week cash forecast**

Build a simple sheet with thirteen columns, one per week.

Rows: opening cash, money coming in, money going out, closing cash.

Fill in the next thirteen weeks as honestly as you can, including the payments
you know are coming that you have been pretending are not.

Then find the week with the lowest closing balance. That is your danger week.
Plan for it now.

**Time:** 90 minutes. Update it every Friday.
:::

# Week 11 — Debt: The Tool and the Trap

Debt is neither good nor evil. It is a multiplier. Applied to a productive asset
with a known return, it accelerates. Applied to consumption or to a business with
no margin, it accelerates the other way.

The test is arithmetic, not morality: will the thing you are borrowing for
generate more cash than the debt costs, soon enough to service it?

::: scripture
"The rich rules over the poor, and the borrower is servant to the lender."
— Proverbs 22:7
:::

Servant is the operative word. Debt buys you money and sells you some of your
freedom. Sometimes that is a good trade. Often it is not.

## The principle

**Borrow only for something that produces cash faster than the debt consumes it —
and never for consumption.**

## The task

::: exercise
**Week 11 task — Debt triage**

1. List every debt: amount, interest rate, monthly payment, purpose.
2. Calculate the total monthly cost.
3. Rank by interest rate, highest first.
4. Write a payoff order and the date each will be cleared.
5. Identify one thing you are currently paying interest on that produces nothing,
   and stop it this month.

**Time:** 60 minutes.
:::

# Week 12 — Build a Buffer

The difference between people who survive shocks and people who are destroyed by
them is almost always a buffer. Not intelligence, not connections — a cushion.

A buffer converts a crisis into an inconvenience. Without one, every crisis
becomes an emergency, and emergencies force bad decisions: selling at a loss,
borrowing at a terrible rate, accepting work that harms you.

::: scripture
"There is desirable treasure, and oil in the dwelling of the wise, but a foolish
man squanders it."
— Proverbs 21:20
:::

## The principle

**A buffer is not savings. It is the thing that stops a bad month from becoming a
collapse.**

## The task

::: exercise
**Week 12 task — Start the buffer**

1. Calculate one month of your essential fixed costs. That is your target.
2. Open a separate account or wallet. Separate. Not mixed with daily money.
3. Set an automatic transfer on the day money arrives — before you spend anything.
4. Start with any amount. The mechanism matters more than the amount.
5. Do not touch it except for genuine emergencies, and write down what counts.

**Time:** 30 minutes to set up.
:::

# Week 13 — The Reinvestment Rule

Growth requires reinvestment. But reinvestment without a rule becomes either
stagnation or reckless expansion, depending on your temperament.

The rule that works: a fixed percentage of every payment goes to the business
before it goes to you. Not what is left over. A percentage, taken first.

::: scripture
"Honour the Lord with your possessions, and with the firstfruits of all your
increase."
— Proverbs 3:9
:::

Firstfruits. Taken first, not from the remainder. The principle is older than
business books and it works for the same reason: what is taken last is never
taken.

## The principle

**Pay the business first, yourself second, and only from what remains.**

## The task

::: exercise
**Week 13 task — Set the percentages**

1. Decide three percentages of every payment you receive: reinvestment, buffer,
   your own pay.
2. Write them down. Start conservative: 20 / 10 / 70 is a reasonable beginning.
3. Apply them to your next three payments, physically separating the money.
4. Review after one month. Adjust once, then leave it alone.

**Time:** 30 minutes.
:::

# Week 14 — Know Your Break-Even

Break-even is the most useful number in business and the one most owners have
never calculated. It answers a single question: how much must I sell before I earn
anything?

Below that line, every sale still helps. Above it, every sale is profit. Knowing
where the line is changes how you feel about a slow week, and how you price.

::: scripture
"For which of you, intending to build a tower, does not sit down first and count
the cost?"
— Luke 14:28
:::

## The principle

**Know the revenue at which you make nothing.** It is the floor beneath your
decisions.

## The task

::: exercise
**Week 14 task — Calculate break-even**

1. Total your monthly fixed costs: rent, salaries, subscriptions, loan payments.
2. Calculate your gross margin percentage: (revenue − direct costs) ÷ revenue.
3. Break-even = fixed costs ÷ gross margin.
4. Write the number where you will see it.
5. Calculate how many units or jobs that represents. That is your monthly target.

**Time:** 45 minutes.
:::

# Week 15 — Separate the Money

The single most common cause of small-business failure is mixing business and
personal money. It makes the business unmeasurable, which makes every decision a
guess.

Separation is not bureaucracy. It is the difference between knowing and hoping.

::: scripture
"Let all things be done decently and in order."
— 1 Corinthians 14:40
:::

## The principle

**Money that is mixed cannot be managed.** Separate accounts, separate records,
separate decisions.

## The task

::: exercise
**Week 15 task — Separate**

1. Open a dedicated account or wallet for the business, if you do not have one.
2. Move all business income there from today.
3. Pay yourself a fixed amount on a fixed date, as a salary.
4. Record every business expense from the business account, however small.
5. At month end, compare revenue, expenses and your salary. That is your actual
   profit or loss.

**Time:** 60 minutes, then a permanent habit.
:::

# Week 16 — Read Your Own Numbers

A business owner who cannot read their own accounts is flying a plane without
instruments. It can work in clear weather. It fails in cloud.

You do not need accounting qualifications. You need four statements understood at
a basic level, and one habit: reading them monthly.

::: scripture
"The heart of him who has understanding seeks knowledge, but the mouth of fools
feeds on foolishness."
— Proverbs 15:14
:::

## The principle

**Understand four numbers monthly: revenue, gross margin, cash, and receivables.**

## The task

::: exercise
**Week 16 task — The monthly read**

1. Produce, from your records, last month's: total revenue, direct costs, fixed
   costs, and closing cash.
2. Calculate gross margin and net profit.
3. List money owed to you, with ages (30, 60, 90+ days).
4. Write three sentences: what improved, what worsened, what you will do.
5. Repeat on the same date every month.

**Time:** 60 minutes monthly.
:::

# Part Three — Systems

*Weeks 17–24: building things that work without you in the room.*

# Week 17 — Document What You Do

If a process lives only in your head, the business dies when you are unavailable,
and it cannot be delegated, and it cannot be improved. Documentation is not
administration. It is the act of turning your labour into an asset.

::: scripture
"Write the vision and make it plain on tablets, that he may run who reads it."
— Habakkuk 2:2
:::

Make it plain, so that the one who reads it can run. That is a job description for
every business owner.

## The principle

**Anything done more than twice should be written down.**

## The task

::: exercise
**Week 17 task — Document three processes**

Choose the three things you do most often. For each, write one page:

1. **Trigger** — what starts this process.
2. **Steps** — numbered, in order, specific enough to follow.
3. **Quality check** — how to know it was done correctly.
4. **Escalate to** — who to ask when it goes wrong.

Then hand one to someone else and watch them follow it. Every question they ask
is a gap.

**Time:** 90 minutes.
:::

# Week 18 — Build the Machine, Not the Heroics

Heroic effort feels virtuous and is a symptom of a broken system. If the business
only works when you work late, the system is failing, and you are absorbing the
failure with your body.

::: scripture
"The horse is prepared for the day of battle, but deliverance is of the Lord."
— Proverbs 21:31
:::

Prepare the horse. Do the work of preparation, and then trust. What you cannot do
is substitute your exhaustion for preparation.

## The principle

**Replace repeated heroics with a designed process.** If you had to rescue it
three times, it needs a system, not more effort.

## The task

::: exercise
**Week 18 task — Find the heroics**

1. List the three things that went wrong this month and required you personally.
2. For each, ask: what would have prevented this?
3. Write the prevention as a rule, a check, or an alert.
4. Implement the cheapest one this week.
5. Note the next time it would have happened, and confirm it did not.

**Time:** 60 minutes.
:::

# Week 19 — Checklists Beat Memory

The most reliable people in the world — pilots, surgeons — use checklists. Not
because they are forgetful, but because memory fails under pressure, and pressure
is exactly when reliability matters.

::: scripture
"Let all things be done properly and in order."
— 1 Corinthians 14:40
:::

## The principle

**A checklist outperforms memory precisely when you are tired, rushed or
distracted — which is when mistakes are made.**

## The task

::: exercise
**Week 19 task — Write one checklist**

1. Choose the process with the most expensive mistakes.
2. Write the checklist: no more than nine items, each a single action.
3. Include the checks that catch the most likely error.
4. Use it three times yourself, then hand it to someone else.
5. Remove any item that was never useful.

**Time:** 45 minutes.
:::

# Week 20 — Measure What Matters

What gets measured improves. What gets measured badly improves in the wrong
direction. Most small businesses measure nothing, or measure what is easy rather
than what matters.

::: scripture
"By faith we understand…" but also "Let no one deceive himself."
— Hebrews 11:3; 1 Corinthians 3:18
:::

Faith is for the future. Numbers are for the present. Do not confuse the two.

## The principle

**Three to five measures, reviewed weekly, beat a hundred reviewed never.**

## The task

::: exercise
**Week 20 task — Choose your measures**

1. List every number you could measure.
2. Choose three that, if they improved, would mean the business improved.
3. For each, define exactly how it is calculated and where it comes from.
4. Build a one-page weekly sheet.
5. Fill it in this Friday, even if the data is incomplete.

**Time:** 45 minutes.
:::

# Week 21 — Delegate, Do Not Abandon

Delegation fails when the owner hands over a task without handing over the
authority, the context, or the standard. The result is either a mess or a
frightened employee asking about everything.

::: scripture
"And the things that you have heard from me among many witnesses, commit these to
faithful men who will be able to teach others also."
— 2 Timothy 2:2
:::

Commit to faithful people who can teach others. That is a chain, not a handover.
Build the chain.

## The principle

**Delegate the outcome, the authority, the standard and the check — all four, or
none.**

## The task

::: exercise
**Week 21 task — Delegate one thing properly**

1. Choose one task you should not be doing.
2. Write: the outcome, the deadline, the standard, the limit of authority, and
   when you will check.
3. Have the conversation. Give the document.
4. Check at the agreed time, not before. Resist the urge to hover.
5. Fix the document, not the person, when something goes wrong.

**Time:** 60 minutes.
:::

# Week 22 — Hire for Character, Train for Skill

Skill can be taught in weeks. Character cannot be taught at all. Most hiring
mistakes are made by prioritising a CV over a pattern of behaviour.

::: scripture
"Moreover you shall select from all the people able men, such as fear God, men of
truth, hating covetousness."
— Exodus 18:21
:::

Note the criteria: able, God-fearing, truthful, not greedy. Only one of those is a
skill.

## The principle

**Hire for honesty, diligence and teachability. Train everything else.**

## The task

::: exercise
**Week 22 task — Write your hiring standard**

1. Write the four character traits you will not compromise on.
2. Write three interview questions that reveal each trait — questions about past
   behaviour, not hypotheticals.
3. Write one reference-check question that reveals honesty.
4. Use this standard for your next hire, even if it slows you down.

**Time:** 45 minutes.
:::

# Week 23 — Fire Quickly, Kindly, Legally

Keeping the wrong person is a decision to harm everyone else: the customers who
receive poor work, and the colleagues who carry the load. Kindness that avoids a
necessary conversation is not kindness.

::: scripture
"Faithful are the wounds of a friend, but the kisses of an enemy are deceitful."
— Proverbs 27:6
:::

## The principle

**Address performance early, honestly, and in writing. If it does not change,
end it — promptly and with dignity.**

## The task

::: exercise
**Week 23 task — The conversation script**

1. Write the script for a performance conversation: the specific behaviour, its
   specific effect, the specific change required, the date you will review.
2. Deliver it to whoever needs to hear it, this week.
3. Put the review date in the calendar.
4. If there is no one, write the script anyway. You will need it.

**Time:** 45 minutes.
:::

# Week 24 — Build the Second Line

A business with one person who can do a critical task is fragile. The test of a
system is whether it survives the absence of any single individual — including
you.

::: scripture
"Two are better than one, because they have a good reward for their labour."
— Ecclesiastes 4:9
:::

## The principle

**Every critical function needs two people who can perform it.**

## The task

::: exercise
**Week 24 task — Find the single points of failure**

1. List every function that would stop if one person were unavailable for a month.
2. Rank by how much damage the stoppage would cause.
3. Take the top one. Train a second person this month.
4. Test it: have them do it while the primary person is absent.
5. Repeat quarterly.

**Time:** 60 minutes, plus training time.
:::

# Part Four — People

*Weeks 25–32: leading, without manipulation or fear.*

# Week 25 — Authority Is Borrowed

Authority in an organisation is not taken from a title. It is lent by the people
who choose to follow. A title gives you the right to give instructions; it does
not give you the ability to be obeyed well.

::: scripture
"And whoever desires to become great among you shall be your servant."
— Mark 10:43
:::

## The principle

**Lead by making others more capable, not more dependent.**

## The task

::: exercise
**Week 25 task — The dependency audit**

1. List the people who report to you or depend on you.
2. For each, write one thing they currently need you for that they could do
   themselves.
3. Choose one. Teach it to them this week.
4. Notice how it feels. That discomfort is the habit you are breaking.

**Time:** 60 minutes, plus the teaching.
:::

# Week 26 — Say the Difficult Thing

Most organisational problems are caused by conversations that were avoided.
Everyone knows, nobody says, and the problem grows in the silence.

::: scripture
"Speaking the truth in love."
— Ephesians 4:15
:::

Truth *and* love. Truth without love is cruelty. Love without truth is cowardice.

## The principle

**Say the difficult thing early, specifically, and privately.**

## The task

::: exercise
**Week 26 task — Have the conversation**

1. Identify one thing you have been avoiding saying.
2. Write it as: the behaviour, the effect, the request.
3. Remove all adjectives. Remove all "always" and "never".
4. Say it this week, privately, in person.
5. Write down what happened. It is almost never as bad as the fear.

**Time:** 30 minutes preparation.
:::

# Week 27 — Pay People Properly

Paying badly is expensive. It produces turnover, resentment, theft, and the quiet
departure of your best people, who leave first because they have options.

::: scripture
"Behold, the wages of the labourers who mowed your fields, which you kept back by
fraud, cry out."
— James 5:4
:::

## The principle

**Pay at or above the market for the people you want to keep, and be explicit
about how they can earn more.**

## The task

::: exercise
**Week 27 task — Check your pay**

1. Find out what the market pays for each role you employ.
2. Compare. Note anyone you are paying below market.
3. Decide: raise, or restructure the role honestly.
4. Write a clear progression: what must a person demonstrate to earn more?
5. Tell them.

**Time:** 60 minutes.
:::

# Week 28 — Build Trust With Consistency

Trust is not built by grand gestures. It is built by the same behaviour repeated
until people can predict it. Predictability is the foundation of safety, and
safety is the foundation of good work.

::: scripture
"A friend loves at all times, and a brother is born for adversity."
— Proverbs 17:17
:::

## The principle

**Be the same person on a good day and a bad day.** Consistency is the currency of
leadership.

## The task

::: exercise
**Week 28 task — The consistency audit**

1. Ask three people who work with you: "What is unpredictable about working with
   me?" Ask genuinely, and do not defend.
2. Write what they say.
3. Choose the one that costs others the most, and fix it for a month.
4. Ask again.

**Time:** 45 minutes.
:::

# Week 29 — Handle Failure Without Blame

Blame teaches people to hide problems, which is the opposite of what you need.
The organisations that survive are the ones where bad news travels fastest.

::: scripture
"Confess your trespasses to one another, and pray for one another, that you may
be healed."
— James 5:16
:::

## The principle

**Punish concealment, not error.** A mistake reported is a lesson. A mistake
hidden is a time bomb.

## The task

::: exercise
**Week 29 task — Change the response**

1. Recall the last time someone brought you a problem. What did you do?
2. Write the response you wish you had given.
3. The next time a problem arrives, use the three questions: "What happened? What
   did we learn? What will prevent it?"
4. Notice whether people start bringing problems earlier.

**Time:** 30 minutes, then a permanent change.
:::

# Week 30 — Mentor Someone

You do not need to be finished to help someone who is behind you. In fact, the
person one step behind you is the one you can help most, because you remember
exactly what that step felt like.

::: scripture
"As iron sharpens iron, so a man sharpens the countenance of his friend."
— Proverbs 27:17
:::

## The principle

**Teach what you just learned.** The teaching consolidates the learning and
multiplies the value.

## The task

::: exercise
**Week 30 task — Take one person**

1. Identify one person who is one step behind you.
2. Offer one hour a month, for six months. Be specific about what you will help
   with.
3. In the first session, ask what they are stuck on, and help with only that.
4. Keep every appointment. Reliability is the whole gift.

**Time:** One hour a month.
:::

# Week 31 — Build a Table, Not a Throne

Nobody makes good decisions alone. The person who has no one who can disagree with
them is not powerful; they are exposed.

::: scripture
"Where there is no counsel, the people fall; but in the multitude of counselors
there is safety."
— Proverbs 11:14
:::

## The principle

**Surround yourself with people who can tell you no, and make it safe for them to
do so.**

## The task

::: exercise
**Week 31 task — Assemble the table**

1. List the three decisions you most need help with.
2. For each, name a person with relevant experience who is not dependent on you.
3. Approach them with a specific ask: "Would you review this decision once a
   quarter?"
4. Hold the first meeting. Bring the numbers. Listen without defending.
5. Write down what they said, and what you did about it.

**Time:** 90 minutes.
:::

# Week 32 — Succession Is Not Betrayal

A thing that cannot continue without you is not an institution. It is a job you
have made for yourself, and it will end when you do.

::: scripture
"David… served his own generation by the will of God, fell asleep."
— Acts 13:36
:::

Served his own generation. That is the whole assignment: serve yours, then let it
continue.

## The principle

**Build so that the thing you started outlives your involvement.**

## The task

::: exercise
**Week 32 task — Write the succession note**

1. Write, in one page, what someone would need to know to run your work without
   you: the key relationships, the decisions only you currently make, the
   unfinished business, the risks.
2. Name a person who could do it, and what they would need to learn.
3. Start teaching them one thing this month.
4. Update the note every year.

**Time:** 90 minutes.
:::

# Part Five — The Long Game

*Weeks 33–44: compounding, adversity, and the disciplines that hold over decades.*

# Week 33 — Compounding Is the Only Magic

Nothing in this book produces a fast result. Everything in it produces a large
result over time. The people who fail are not those who chose wrong; they are
those who stopped.

::: scripture
"Precept must be upon precept, precept upon precept, line upon line, line upon
line; here a little, there a little."
— Isaiah 28:10
:::

## The principle

**Choose things that accumulate, then do not interrupt them.**

## The task

::: exercise
**Week 33 task — Identify your compounding assets**

1. List everything you are doing that accumulates: skills, relationships,
   reputation, savings, systems, health.
2. List everything that does not: busywork, one-off favours, distractions.
3. Cut one non-compounding activity this week.
4. Protect one compounding activity with a calendar appointment.

**Time:** 45 minutes.
:::

# Week 34 — Adversity Is Curriculum

Every person you admire has been through something that should have stopped them.
What separated them was not the absence of the difficulty but the interpretation
of it.

::: scripture
"And we know that all things work together for good to those who love God, to
those who are the called according to His purpose."
— Romans 8:28
:::

All things. Not the pleasant things.

## The principle

**Ask "what is this teaching me?" before asking "why is this happening to me?"**

## The task

::: exercise
**Week 34 task — The adversity ledger**

1. List the three hardest things you have been through.
2. For each, write one capability you now have because of it.
3. Write one thing you would do differently, and one thing you would not change.
4. Read it the next time something goes wrong. It will be true again.

**Time:** 45 minutes.
:::

# Week 35 — Guard Your Body

The work requires a body. Every plan in this book assumes you are alive and
functioning. Sleep, food and movement are not luxuries; they are the infrastructure
of everything else.

::: scripture
"Do you not know that your body is the temple of the Holy Spirit…? You were bought
at a price; therefore glorify God in your body."
— 1 Corinthians 6:19–20
:::

## The principle

**Health is not a reward for success. It is a prerequisite.**

## The task

::: exercise
**Week 35 task — The three non-negotiables**

1. Choose a sleep time and keep it for seven days. Not a duration — a time.
2. Choose one form of movement you will do three times this week. Walking counts.
3. Choose one food change you can sustain. One, not five.
4. Mark each day. Do not aim for perfection; aim for the pattern.

**Time:** 10 minutes a day.
:::

# Week 36 — Guard Your Mind

What you consume shapes what you can imagine. An hour of scrolling produces a
different mind from an hour of reading, and the difference compounds as surely as
money does.

::: scripture
"Finally, brethren, whatever things are true, whatever things are noble, whatever
things are just, whatever things are pure, whatever things are lovely, whatever
things are of good report… meditate on these things."
— Philippians 4:8
:::

## The principle

**Your inputs determine your ceiling.** Choose them deliberately.

## The task

::: exercise
**Week 36 task — The input audit**

1. For three days, note everything you consume: social media, news, conversation,
   entertainment.
2. Mark each: builds, neutral, drains.
3. Remove one drain permanently.
4. Add one build: twenty pages a day, or thirty minutes of learning.

**Time:** 3 days of noting, 30 minutes of analysis.
:::

# Week 37 — Keep Your Word to Yourself

The promises you break most often are the ones nobody sees. And those are the ones
that determine whether you can trust your own plans.

::: scripture
"Better not to vow than to vow and not pay."
— Ecclesiastes 5:5
:::

## The principle

**Self-trust is built the same way trust is: small promises, kept.**

## The task

::: exercise
**Week 37 task — One promise**

1. Make one promise to yourself, small enough to be certain.
2. Write it down with a date.
3. Keep it for seven days. Mark each day.
4. On day eight, make another. Slightly larger.
5. Repeat for the rest of the year.

**Time:** 5 minutes a day.
:::

# Week 38 — Learn From People Who Disagree

Agreement is comfortable and useless. The people who will change your thinking are
the ones who see the same facts and reach a different conclusion.

::: scripture
"Let the wise hear and increase in learning."
— Proverbs 1:5
:::

## The principle

**Seek out the strongest version of the view you do not hold.**

## The task

::: exercise
**Week 38 task — Steel-man an opposite view**

1. Choose a belief you hold strongly about your work.
2. Find someone who disagrees, and ask them to argue their case properly.
3. Write their argument in its strongest form, as if you believed it.
4. Write what would have to be true for them to be right.
5. Check whether any of it is true.

**Time:** 60 minutes.
:::

# Week 39 — Do the Boring Things First

The interesting work is a small fraction of any real job. The people who succeed
are those who do the boring things reliably and quickly, so that they have time
for the interesting part.

::: scripture
"Whatever you do, do it heartily, as to the Lord and not to men."
— Colossians 3:23
:::

## The principle

**Do the unpleasant task first, before the day has a chance to fill itself.**

## The task

::: exercise
**Week 39 task — The first hour**

1. Identify the task you most avoid that most needs doing.
2. Do it first, for the next five working days, before anything else.
3. Note what happens to the rest of the day.
4. Continue if it works. It usually does.

**Time:** One hour a day, five days.
:::

# Week 40 — Build the Year, Not the Day

Daily effort without direction produces motion. Annual direction without daily
effort produces fantasy. You need both, in that order.

::: scripture
"To everything there is a season, a time for every purpose under heaven."
— Ecclesiastes 3:1
:::

## The principle

**Set the year's three outcomes, then work backwards to this week.**

## The task

::: exercise
**Week 40 task — The annual three**

1. Write three outcomes you want to be true in twelve months.
2. For each, write the quarterly milestones.
3. For this quarter, write the monthly steps.
4. For this month, write this week's actions.
5. Keep only the ones you will actually do.

**Time:** 90 minutes.
:::

# Week 41 — Say Thank You

Gratitude is not soft. It is accurate. Almost nothing you have was built alone,
and the people who helped you are still watching whether you remember.

::: scripture
"In everything give thanks, for this is the will of God in Christ Jesus for you."
— 1 Thessalonians 5:18
:::

## The principle

**Name the people who made your progress possible, and tell them.**

## The task

::: exercise
**Week 41 task — Five messages**

Write five messages to five people who helped you and probably do not know it.

Specific. What they did, and what it made possible. No request attached.

Send them this week.

**Time:** 45 minutes.
:::

# Week 42 — Rest Is a Strategy

A machine that runs without stopping breaks. So does a person. Rest is not the
absence of work; it is what makes the work sustainable and the judgement sound.

::: scripture
"And He said to them, 'Come aside by yourselves to a deserted place and rest a
while.'"
— Mark 6:31
:::

## The principle

**Schedule rest before you need it, and protect it like a client appointment.**

## The task

::: exercise
**Week 42 task — The weekly stop**

1. Choose one full day a week with no work. Write it in the calendar.
2. Choose one evening a week that ends at a fixed hour.
3. Tell the people who need to know.
4. Keep it for four weeks. Notice what changes in your thinking.

**Time:** One day a week.
:::

# Week 43 — Teach It to Keep It

Knowledge you can explain is knowledge you have. Knowledge you cannot explain is
recognition, which fades.

::: scripture
"And the things that you have heard from me… commit these to faithful men who
will be able to teach others also."
— 2 Timothy 2:2
:::

## The principle

**Teach what you know. The teaching is how you learn it properly.**

## The task

::: exercise
**Week 43 task — Write the guide**

1. Choose one thing you do well.
2. Write a one-page guide: what it is, when to use it, how to do it, common
   mistakes.
3. Give it to someone who does not know the thing.
4. Watch them use it. Fix the guide where they get stuck.

**Time:** 60 minutes.
:::

# Week 44 — Give It Away

There is a point where holding on stops being prudence and becomes fear. Generosity
is not the opposite of building; it is the proof that what you built is real.

::: scripture
"Give, and it will be given to you: good measure, pressed down, shaken together,
and running over."
— Luke 6:38
:::

## The principle

**Give a fixed proportion, first, before you feel you can afford it.** What is
given last is never given.

## The task

::: exercise
**Week 44 task — Set the proportion**

1. Decide a percentage you will give, permanently.
2. Take it first, when money arrives, before spending.
3. Give it to something you actually believe in, not to whoever asks loudest.
4. Do it for three months before deciding whether to change the amount.

**Time:** 30 minutes.
:::

# Part Six — Legacy

*Weeks 45–52: what you leave, and who you become.*

# Week 45 — Decide What You Are For

A person without a purpose will be employed by someone else's. This is not a
criticism of employment; it is a warning about drift.

::: scripture
"For we are His workmanship, created in Christ Jesus for good works, which God
prepared beforehand that we should walk in them."
— Ephesians 2:10
:::

## The principle

**Write down what you are for, in one sentence, and test your calendar against
it.**

## The task

::: exercise
**Week 45 task — The sentence**

1. Write, in one sentence, what you want your work to have been for.
2. Test it: does your calendar this week reflect it? Does your spending?
3. Rewrite it until it is honest.
4. Put it where you will see it every morning.

**Time:** 60 minutes.
:::

# Week 46 — Build Institutions, Not Empires

An empire depends on one person. An institution outlives them. The difference is
whether you built systems, values and successors, or only power.

::: scripture
"Unless the Lord builds the house, they labour in vain who build it."
— Psalm 127:1
:::

## The principle

**Build something that would continue if you disappeared tomorrow.**

## The task

::: exercise
**Week 46 task — The disappearance test**

1. Write what would happen to your work if you were unavailable for six months.
2. List what would fail.
3. Choose the most damaging item and fix it this month: document it, train
   someone, or remove the dependency.
4. Repeat annually.

**Time:** 60 minutes.
:::

# Week 47 — Guard Your Integrity in Small Things

Integrity is not tested in the large decisions. It is built in the small ones,
and then it holds when the large ones arrive.

::: scripture
"He who is faithful in what is least is faithful also in much; and he who is
unjust in what is least is unjust also in much."
— Luke 16:10
:::

## The principle

**Small compromises are not small.** They are rehearsals.

## The task

::: exercise
**Week 47 task — The small test**

1. List the small dishonesties available to you: the rounded invoice, the
   exaggerated claim, the expense that is nearly legitimate.
2. Choose one you have been tolerating, and stop.
3. Write the rule you will follow instead.
4. Tell one person the rule, so it is not only in your head.

**Time:** 45 minutes.
:::

# Week 48 — Prepare the Next Generation

Whatever you learn and do not pass on dies with you. This is the difference between
a career and a contribution.

::: scripture
"One generation shall praise Your works to another, and shall declare Your mighty
acts."
— Psalm 145:4
:::

## The principle

**Your knowledge is not complete until it is in someone else's hands.**

## The task

::: exercise
**Week 48 task — Take on an apprentice**

1. Identify one young person who is willing to work.
2. Offer a specific arrangement: what they will learn, what they will do, for how
   long.
3. Give them real work, not errands.
4. Review weekly. Correct specifically. Praise specifically.
5. Do not rescue them from every difficulty. That is how they learn.

**Time:** Ongoing.
:::

# Week 49 — Choose Your Successor Before You Need To

Succession planned in a crisis is not succession; it is improvisation under
pressure. The best time to identify who comes next is when you do not need them.

::: scripture
"And the things that you have heard from me among many witnesses, commit these to
faithful men."
— 2 Timothy 2:2
:::

## The principle

**Name your successor early, and start preparing them now.**

## The task

::: exercise
**Week 49 task — Name them**

1. Write the name of the person who could take your role, or the closest thing to
   it.
2. Write what they would need to learn, and what experience would teach it.
3. Give them one piece of it this month.
4. Tell them, explicitly, that you are preparing them. People rise to expectations
   that are stated.

**Time:** 60 minutes.
:::

# Week 50 — Measure Your Life, Not Just Your Business

A profitable business run by a destroyed person is a failure with good numbers.
The measure that matters includes your family, your health, your faith, and your
peace.

::: scripture
"For what profit is it to a man if he gains the whole world, and loses his own
soul?"
— Matthew 16:26
:::

## The principle

**Include the unmeasured things in your accounting, or they will be spent without
your noticing.**

## The task

::: exercise
**Week 50 task — The whole ledger**

1. Write five columns: work, family, health, faith, community.
2. Score each honestly out of ten, today.
3. Write which one has been sacrificed for the others.
4. Choose one action this week that raises the lowest score. One.
5. Repeat quarterly.

**Time:** 45 minutes.
:::

# Week 51 — Finish Well

Most people do not fail at the start. They fail near the end, when the novelty is
gone and the discipline is tired. Finishing is a separate skill from starting, and
it must be practised.

::: scripture
"I have fought the good fight, I have finished the race, I have kept the faith."
— 2 Timothy 4:7
:::

## The principle

**Finish what you started, or end it deliberately.** Do not let things drift into
abandonment.

## The task

::: exercise
**Week 51 task — Close the open loops**

1. List every unfinished thing: projects, promises, courses, conversations.
2. For each, decide: finish, delegate, or end it.
3. Do the ending ones this week — properly, with a message, not by silence.
4. Choose one to finish and set a date.

**Time:** 60 minutes, plus the messages.
:::

# Week 52 — Begin Again

A year of principles is not a destination. It is a foundation, and foundations are
for building on.

::: scripture
"Brethren, I do not count myself to have apprehended; but one thing I do,
forgetting those things which are behind and reaching forward to those things
which are ahead, I press toward the goal."
— Philippians 3:13–14
:::

## The principle

**Review the year honestly, keep what worked, and set the next year's three.**

## The task

::: exercise
**Week 52 task — The annual review**

1. Read your notebook from the year. Every week's task and what happened.
2. Write: what worked, what did not, what you would tell yourself a year ago.
3. Write the three outcomes for the coming year.
4. Choose three principles from this book to repeat, because you did not do them
   properly.
5. Begin again.

**Time:** 3 hours. Do it properly. It is the most valuable three hours of the
year.
:::

# Afterword — The Position

I called this book *From Scratch to President* because *president* is the word we
use for the person who presides — the one who stands at the front, not because
they seized the position, but because they built the thing that needed presiding
over.

That position is not a salary. It is a standing: the standing to decide your own
days, to refuse work that harms you, to protect the people who depend on you, to
speak with weight in your community, and to leave something standing when you go.

Nobody gives it to you. It is assembled, week by week, from small promises kept,
prices held, systems built, people developed and honest numbers faced.

::: scripture
"His lord said to him, 'Well done, good and faithful servant; you were faithful
over a few things, I will make you ruler over many things. Enter into the joy of
your lord.'"
— Matthew 25:21
:::

Faithful over a few things. That is the whole method. Not brilliant over many
things, not lucky over the right things — faithful over the few things that are
actually in your hands this week.

Start where you are. Do the task. Come back next week.

::: prayer
**A closing prayer**

Lord, You who build all things: give me the patience to lay one stone properly
rather than to describe a cathedral I will never build. Give me honest scales,
clean hands, and a heart that does not despise small beginnings.

Let the work of my hands be useful to someone. Let the people who depend on me be
safe. And when I am finished, let what I built still be standing, serving people I
will never meet.

Amen.
:::
